# Billing

FrockBot sells three account plans: **BYO**, US$5 per month with no usage credit, for people who bring their own models and Computer; **Standard**, US$20 per month including US$20 of usage credit; and **Plus**, US$50 per month including US$60. The markup is in the rates (see [Prices and money](#prices-and-money)), so a plan includes as much usage as it costs, and Plus more. The monthly allowance expires at period end and is consumed before purchased credit. An account's first Standard or Plus subscription starts with a 7-day trial carrying US$3 of credit; BYO has none. Top-ups are US$10, US$25 or US$50, carry forward, and require a paid subscription to spend. Billing is ordinary app code, not a runtime Plugin.

## Plans and the trial

What a deployment sells is its payments Package's `PaymentsPlanV1` (`core/contracts/payments-package.ts`): a list of monthly `subscriptions`, cheapest first, and an optional `trial`. The Stripe Package's is `STRIPE_PLAN_V1` in `app/payments/stripe/stripe.ts`. Each plan is its own monthly Stripe price: `STRIPE_BYO_PRICE_ID` for BYO, `STRIPE_MONTHLY_PRICE_ID` for Standard and `STRIPE_PLUS_PRICE_ID` for Plus. A deployment without the BYO or the Plus price offers no action for that plan, and its checkout and plan change refuse it. A subscription and each paid period name their `planId`, and the ledger grants that plan's allowance. `GET /api/billing` answers the deployment's `plan`, the account's `subscription` (with `planId` and `trialEnd`), `trial: { endsAt, creditMicros }` while one runs, and `actions`, each `subscribe` or `change-plan` action naming its `plan`. A gauge of included credit reads `includedMicros` against `includedGrantedMicros`, every live monthly allowance as granted: after a plan change the old month's allowance runs beside the new one, so the current plan's figure is not the full mark. While `trial` is set, its `creditMicros` is.

**The trial.** Checkout takes the card up front and, for an account that has never subscribed or trialled, asks Stripe for a 7-day trial. Stripe opens a trial with an invoice marked paid for nothing. The Package answers it with the ledger port's `grantTrial`: `trial:<subscriptionId>`, the plan's trial credit as complimentary credit expiring when the trial does. It never grants the allowance or a paid period, so during the trial the account is not `subscribed`, can spend only that credit, and cannot buy top-ups. The ledger marks the account as having had its trial (`trialUsed`), and refuses a second. On day 8 Stripe charges the first month, and that invoice grants the allowance as any renewal does. The first paid period ends the trial's credit at its start, so a trial ended early does not leave its credit running beside the allowance.

**Changing plan.** `POST /api/billing/provider/plan` `{ id, plan }`, the `change-plan` action, moves a subscription and answers the Billing page's URL. An upgrade replaces the subscription's price with `billing_cycle_anchor=now`, `proration_behavior=none` and `payment_behavior=error_if_incomplete`: the new plan is charged in full at once and the billing month restarts, so its invoice (`subscription_update`) grants the new allowance and a new paid period. Credit left from the old month keeps its own expiry. A downgrade replaces the price with no proration and no new anchor, so nothing is charged until renewal, which invoices the lower plan. During a trial, either plan ends the trial now (`trial_end=now`) and charges that plan's first month; this is how someone whose trial credit ran out starts paying early. Nothing prorates, which the ledger does not model. The command's intent is recorded before Stripe is called and the request carries a stable idempotency key; past 23 hours an unresolved change goes to reconciliation rather than risk a second charge.

An invoice grants the allowance of the plan whose price its subscription line names, and only when the line's amount is that plan's monthly price; tax sits outside the line.

**BYO.** A plan may say `trial: false`, and BYO does: its checkout never asks Stripe for a trial, it does not spend the account's one trial, and the ledger refuses trial credit for a subscription on it. It includes no usage credit, so its paid month grants no allowance. What it covers:

- **Hosting.**
- **Jev, with fair use up to US$2 a month.** A plan's `jevFairUseMicros` is granted with each paid period as a `jev` grant (`jev:<subscriptionId>:<periodStart>`), expiring with it. Only a `jev` operation may draw on it, and one draws on it before anything else. On such a plan Turn supervision is metered: the Bot object hands the Turn's supervisor a meter that wraps whichever Jev transport the deployment uses, the Workers AI binding or `JEV_BASE_URL` (`JevFetchMeterV1` in `app/supervision/jev.ts`), with `createPlatformJevFetchV1` in `app/billing/jev.ts`, which reserves each Jev request before it is sent, as a `jev` operation marked `platform`, keyed `jev:turn:<uuid>` — each request is its own charge, since a retry is another request Jev counts — and settles it on the input tokens Jev reports, at the terminal's rate. Past the fair use, Jev draws from credit like any other service. With neither, the request is refused before Jev is asked with `JEV_FAIR_USE_EXHAUSTED_REASON_V1`, which ends the Turn and is shown as written: Jev has no permissive failure mode. On a plan without `jevFairUseMicros` a `platform` reservation answers `covered` and records nothing. The other platform judges (compaction choice, routine reports, email triage and the like) stay overhead on every plan.
- **Connected apps through FrockBot's Composio**, which any plan covers, trial included, and no plan does not. The ledger's `requirePlan` refuses an account on no plan with `connectedAppsPlanRequiredReasonV1`: starting a sign-in in the User object, and each connected-app tool call and terminal request in a Turn. A deployment that sells no subscription asks for none, and one that does not bill asks nothing.

Everything else of FrockBot's — its Computer, Frock AI, voice, dictation, search and images — is paid from top-ups at the usual rates, which are spendable on BYO as on any paid subscription. `GET /api/billing` answers `jevFairUse: { remainingMicros, grantedMicros }` on a plan that meters Jev, and the Billing page measures a BYO month against it.

## Implementation status

**The switch is the payments Package's, and Stripe's is `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY`.** A deployment without it ships the billing code switched off: nothing is metered, no subscription is required, the billing page reports that payments are not available, and `scripts/check-billing-release.ts` skips its launch check. Setting the key on a hosted origin turns metering and the subscription requirement on together; a local origin never bills. The four billing values are therefore optional deploy secrets until launch.

The Stripe payments Package, account-owned credit ledger, model and Computer usage accounting, web and native billing screens, and marketing pricing section are implemented locally. Targeted verification runs use Sol, as requested; the final full test/merge/release sequence is reserved for the coordinator’s release slot. **This change is not ready to deploy or accept real payments until Stripe test-mode qualification is complete.** `scripts/check-billing-release.ts` refuses launch without the required Stripe values.

The initial Computer tariff is fixed rather than a claim about measured Fly resource consumption: **US$2.75 per active hour**. That is the current 8-vCPU, 16 GB RAM and 100 GB hot-storage ceiling at Fly's published rates, doubled and rounded up. Viewer time prepays in 30-second blocks: one when the viewer opens and one on each visible-client heartbeat. A viewer call the host refuses — no running desktop, an update in flight, a provider failure — settles its elapsed duration to the second instead of a viewing block, so a refused probe never bills a successful viewer window. Other Computer operations reserve their maximum duration and settle their elapsed duration to the second. A refused viewer renewal revokes the token and stops that Bot's viewer service so its open connection cannot keep the Sprite billable. Cancellation, viewer revocation and control release remain available without credit.

If the Computer host binding fails before returning a response, whether the external effect ran is unknown. Its reservation remains pending for evidence-based reconciliation; the system does not manufacture a charge or refund from a transport error.

Up to 100 GB of existing Sprite storage while idle is included in the subscription. At the current cold-storage rate, the full allowance costs about US$1.97 per month. The subscription margin covers that retained state; it is not deducted continuously from credit.

Hosted image generation has no configured unit-price contract yet and refuses without charging. This is a visible unavailable feature, not a blocker for subscriptions, text models, BYO models or Computer usage. It must receive its own bounded reservation and durable operation key before being offered to billed accounts. Memory embeddings and ordinary application storage/hosting are product overhead covered by the subscription; they are not silently added to a customer's model bill. Memory extraction is not: after a chat Turn, one call on `@frock/structured` reads the person's words for memories and is billed to that Turn as summary spend, like compaction. Jev decisions are product overhead too, at US$0.042 per million input tokens: Turn supervision and every other platform judge, and the calls a Plugin makes through its `jev` grant. The exception to that is Turn supervision on a plan with Jev fair use, such as BYO (see [Plans and the trial](#plans-and-the-trial)). A Plugin's are itemised under it on the Work view, with no cost, and capped at 64 a run. The exception is Jev asked from the Computer's terminal at `jev.internal` (`app/supervision/jev-egress.ts`): the account pays for those at **US$0.084 per million input tokens**, twice Jev's rate, in a `jev` operation keyed `jev:<exec effect id>:jev:<n>` under `pricing_version` `jev-2026-09-27` (`app/billing/jev.ts`). Each request holds its body's bytes plus 1,000 tokens as its ceiling before Jev is asked, settles on the input tokens Jev reports, rounded up to a whole micro-dollar, and never above the hold. A refusal Jev answered releases the hold; a timeout or a dropped connection leaves it reserved for reconciliation. An account that cannot spend is refused and Jev is never asked. The Spending page shows these as Jev decisions.

Web search is platform-paid at a fixed **US$0.01 per search**: Brave Search's flat US$0.005 per request, doubled (`app/billing/search.ts`). Before a Bot's `web_search` asks Brave, it reserves that price as a `search` operation keyed `search:<effect id>`, the search's own durable effect identity, recording the tariff's own `pricing_version` (`search-2026-09-24`) rather than the plan's or a model table's. An answered request settles the charge, a request Brave refuses releases it, and one that got no answer at all — a network failure or a cancelled Turn — stays reserved for reconciliation, because whether Brave counted it is unknown. The tool is idempotent, so recovery after an eviction re-runs a search rather than reading one back; the re-run finds the reservation under the same key and is never billed a second time. An account that cannot spend gets the refusal as the tool's error and Brave is never asked. Search on a provider the person brings — Brave, Exa or Tavily on their own key, or their own SearXNG instance — is never billed and never reserves anything. There is no fallback between the two: when their provider refuses, fails or runs out, the tool returns that error to the model and FrockBot's Brave is not asked.

## The payments Package

The ledger, metering, Spending and the Billing page are FrockBot's and every deployment's. What a deployment chooses at build time is how people pay for credit: a Package behind `PaymentsPackageV1` (`core/contracts/payments-package.ts`), chosen the way the [auth Package](adr/0028-open-deployment.md) is. The profile's `payments` names `stripe`, `none`, or a path to a chooser module a white-label wrote in its own repository ([ADR 0038](adr/0038-white-label-deployments.md) §1); the generator aliases `#payments` to it, and `stripe`, the tracked default (`apps/cloudflare/src/payments.ts`), writes no alias. A chooser exports `PAYMENTS_PACKAGE_V1` and the `PaymentsPackageEnvironmentV1` its settings are read from; an external one's profile names those settings in `paymentsEnvironment`, and its secrets are required by the production-secrets check. `none` (`payments.none.ts`) meters nothing and sells nothing.

A Package declares:

- **The plan** — the monthly subscriptions, each with its price and included credit, or none; the first subscription's trial, if any; the top-up amounts; and whether purchased credit is spendable only while subscribed. The ledger takes it from the Package, and `GET /api/billing` answers it as `plan`. Without a subscription to sell, purchased credit may be spendable on its own, and a metered account that has none is told it has no credit rather than to subscribe.
- **Whether payments are live** — the metering switch, read off `env` (Stripe's: its secret key) — and whether purchases can be made now, which enables the Billing page's actions.
- **Its routes**, every request under `/api/billing/provider/`: the purchases the signed-in person starts, inbound provider events, and any page of its own. A Package may also declare `paths` under `/api/billing/` that are not the app's own, for addresses a provider already delivers to or an installed client already calls. A route runs in the gateway and hands an account's work to that account's User Durable Object as a named command, where the Package's account half runs.
- **The Billing page's actions, as data** — each a purpose (`subscribe`, `change-plan`, `top-up` or `manage`), the plan a `subscribe` or `change-plan` action is for, a label, and a target: a URL the client opens, or a command path it POSTs to as the signed-in person and then opens the URL it answers. Each opens in the browser or a web view in the app, on the hosts the action names or the deployment's own.
- **Account deletion** — whatever the provider holds for an account, so nothing keeps charging a person who no longer has one.

A Package never touches ledger storage. Its account half is handed `PaymentsLedgerPortV1`, which reads the account and keeps the Package's own records beside the ledger — never under the ledger's own keys — and credits it only inside `apply(receipt, evidence, effects)`: one provider event, applied at most once by the provider's own event id, in one transaction with its receipt. The same receipt with different evidence is refused. Inside, a Package may grant purchased credit under its own payment reference (the same reference again grants nothing, and with another amount refuses the whole event), record a subscription's state (which ends it when no longer `active`; an event about an older subscription is refused), grant a subscription's trial credit (once per account; see [Plans and the trial](#plans-and-the-trial)), record a paid period (the ledger grants the named plan's allowance for it once and never moves paid access backwards), and suspend the account. The effects are live only while `apply` runs.

`scripts/check-payments-package-imports.ts` keeps Stripe inside `app/payments/stripe/**` and its chooser, keeps each Package to the contract, `@frockbot/app/billing/errors` and `/wire` and its own files, and keeps provider names out of `app/billing/**`. `apps/cloudflare/tsconfig.payments-none.json` type-checks the Worker against the build that does not bill. The white-label fixture's stub Package credits an account from a test webhook through the port, against the published packages (`bun run build:white-label`) and in this repository's suite.

## Prices and money

All balances use integer micro-US-dollars: US$1 = 1,000,000 units. Stripe prices and payment amounts use integer cents. Provider resource costs and customer charges are separate. Hosted model and proposed computer prices apply a 2× multiplier to the configured standard provider rates. These rates do not include an allocation of shared plan discounts or free provider allowances.

### Hosted model rates

Hosted model prices are one versioned table held by the `DeploymentPolicy` authority (`app/billing/rates.ts`, `apps/cloudflare/src/model-rates.ts`) and edited on the [admin portal](adr/0028-open-deployment.md)'s **Hosted model rates** section. Prices are in micro-dollars per token, which is US dollars per million tokens; fractions such as `0.006` are ordinary. A table has two parts:

- **`routes`** — the model ids a call asks for (`@frock/auto`, `@frock/deepseek-ai/deepseek-v4-flash-0731`, and `@frock/structured`, the `frock-structured` Gateway route every Bot's conversation summaries run on whatever its own model; a pre-rename `@flock/…` id is priced by its own entry, else by its `@frock/` one). The specialists (`@frock/writing`, `@frock/coding`, `@frock/thinking`, `@frock/vision`, each on its own Gateway route named for it (`writing`, `coding`, `thinking`, `vision`)) are optional: a Bot is offered one only once the table prices its route. Each entry carries `inputMicrosPerToken`, `cachedInputMicrosPerToken`, `outputMicrosPerToken`, `maximumInputTokens` and `maximumOutputTokens`, and is a **ceiling**.
- **`served`** — the models the Gateway actually runs, keyed `<cf-aig-provider>/<cf-aig-model>` exactly as its answer headers name them (today `custom-together/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash`), each with the three per-token prices.

A hosted call reserves its requested route's rate at that route's token bounds before anything is sent, and settles at the rate of the model that answered, as the Gateway's `cf-aig-provider` and `cf-aig-model` headers name it. Auto is a dashboard route that can be retargeted without a release, so the served model is the only honest price. The settlement records `servedModel`, the customer `unitRates` it charged, and `pricing`: `served`; `capped` when the served model is priced above its route, so the ceiling was charged and the deployment absorbed the rest; `unpriced` when the table has no rate for the served model, or the answer named none, so the ceiling was charged; or `cached` for a Gateway cache hit, which ran no provider and settles at zero. Every request sends `cf-aig-skip-cache: true`, so a cache hit should not occur. An `unpriced` settlement is also reported to the authority, at most once a minute per model per Bot object, and listed on the admin portal until a version prices that model.

Every save is a new version and no version is ever rewritten. The version becomes each model reservation's `pricing_version` (`model-rates-<n>`), and the reservation keeps the ceiling's unit prices, so a later version cannot rewrite the explanation for a charge. A save names the version it was edited from, and one made over a version someone else saved is refused as a conflict. A table must price `@frock/auto`, since every Bot starts on it, and `@frock/structured`, since every Bot's summaries run on it; a route whose bounds would reserve more than US$500,000 is refused. A deployment with no table is seeded version 1 from the prices production ran on before the table existed; the seed gives `@frock/structured` Auto's ceiling, is receipted as `maintenance:model-rates-seed:2026-09-24` and never overwrites a saved version.

Each Bot object reads the table at most once a minute and prices from that copy; a failed read keeps the copy it holds, and a Bot that has never read one refuses the hosted call without reserving anything. The Gateway holds each hosted request to its own route's `maximumInputTokens`/`maximumOutputTokens`, from the same copy — a request whose Gateway model no route names, such as a structured Auto request pinned to its own model, to the smallest. The public billing response lists each route's customer price, twice its rate, as the most a call to it can cost. Hosted image-containing chat requests are refused until their token bounds can be quoted safely; BYO models continue to support them through their provider.

Computer customer rate: **US$2.75 per active hour**, drawn from the shared prepaid balance. It is a fixed tariff based on the maximum Sprite resource envelope, not measured CPU/RAM/storage consumption. The US$20 Standard allowance buys about 7 hours 16 minutes if spent entirely on Computer time. No fixed number of hours is promised because hosted model charges use the same balance.

## Durable account ledger

The existing User Durable Object owns SQLite tables for grants, operations, provider and administrator receipts, and account state. It validates its User identity before every billing RPC. A balance mutation and its receipt share one synchronous transaction. Distinct Bots reserve against this one authority.

An administrator can grant an account **complimentary credit** by hand from the [admin portal](adr/0028-open-deployment.md). It is a third grant kind beside the monthly allowance and purchased top-ups: an admin's grant never expires (the trial's credit, the same kind, ends with the trial), it is spent after monthly credit and before purchased credit, and it is the one kind spendable **without** a paid subscription. Each grant carries the admin's own idempotency id, the admin's identity and a reason, so a repeated request grants once and a changed amount under the same id conflicts. A single grant is capped at US$1,000. Purchased top-ups still require a subscription to spend.

The account's spend rule, in order: a suspended account is refused; without a subscription, only complimentary credit counts, and none left is refused as "subscription required"; with less spendable credit than the reservation needs, the call is refused as "no usage credit left". Both refusals are written for the person and reach the conversation verbatim (see `app/shell/run-failure-copy.ts`), with an Open Billing action beside them; the Profile page shows the remaining credit at the top, the chat pane banners an account that cannot spend, and the failed-Turn notification carries the same sentence.

Each model dispatch reserves its bounded maximum before the provider is called. A duplicate operation key cannot redispatch the provider, including after eviction. The provider's reported usage, priced at the model that answered (see [Hosted model rates](#hosted-model-rates)), settles the charge and returns unused funds to the original grants; an expired monthly grant never becomes fresh monthly credit. BYO model calls require a spendable account — a paid subscription or remaining complimentary credit — but have zero FrockBot model cost, and their token usage is still recorded.

Metering wraps the registered provider stream inside the Plugin hook chain. A Plugin cannot reduce a bill by replacing the outward usage event. A hook that returns without invoking the provider incurs no provider charge. Missing or uncertain usage remains reserved for reconciliation; byte/token estimates are never billed as provider-reported consumption. A definitive no-effect provider failure releases the reservation.

`GET /api/billing` answers with available monthly, purchased and complimentary credit, pending reservations, the subscription and its period end, the plan, recent usage, recent credit grants and what the account spent in the last 30 days, with who handles payments (`paymentsProvider`) and the actions the payments Package offers this account (`actions`). Usage pagination uses SQLite insertion sequence, so equal timestamps do not skip rows.

## The Billing page

The Flutter app has one **Billing** page, a row on You. It opens on a gauge, not a dollar meter: the share of this month's allowance left (`includedGrantedMicros`, every live monthly allowance as it was granted, is 100% — so a paid month of a bigger plan still counts after a move down, and the old month's credit beside the new one after a move up; the balance's `includedMicros` is what is left), with what is held for work still running as a lighter part of the bar and a tick where an even pace through the billing month would be. Under it one line answers whether the credit lasts: at the last seven days' pace from Spending's `credit`, "Lasts until your plan renews on …", or, in amber with Top up and the move to a plan with more room beside it, "At your pace, runs out around …". An account whose oldest grant is under a week old shows the share and no date. Top-up credit is a line under the gauge — in reserve, used after the month's allowance, never expires — until the allowance is spent, when the reserve in dollars becomes the answer. A trial reads "Trial · N days left" measured against `trial.creditMicros`; a subscribed account with nothing left reads "Paused until …" with Top up and the move up. Dollars appear only where money changes hands: plan prices, top-ups, the reserve, invoices. Below the gauge the deployment's plans sit side by side, described by how much room each gives and with the included credit as small print; a plan is shown only when the account is on it or an action names it. Every button is one of the payments Package's actions, drawn with its label, and the page names no provider: `subscribe` for each plan opens the provider's checkout; `change-plan` is confirmed first and answers the plan it moved to, so Billing refreshes rather than opening a page — up starts now with a new billing month, down starts at renewal, and during a trial either plan ends the trial and starts paying; `top-up` opens an amount picker; `manage` opens the provider's pages, and mends a subscription that lapsed or is past due. An account whose Bots cannot reply without a plan, or one suspended, is told so first, in red. A deployment that sells no subscription shows no plans. Below that is Spending, then a Prices row that opens the Computer rate and the hosted model rates.

## Spending

**Spending** answers where an account's credit went. It is a section of Billing, which search can open scrolled to it. A Bot's settings and a Routine's run log open the same view on a page of its own, narrowed to that Bot or Routine and showing the credit left beside what it cost. Every Bot, Routine and day figure is a share of the account's monthly plan allowance, which can pass 100%; one **Show in dollars** switch, remembered on the device, restores dollars, and an account without a plan reads in dollars. It leads with the answer: what the period cost against the period before, how long the credit lasts at the last week's pace and whether that is past its renewal (on its own page; in Billing the balance says it), and the cause that drove most of it. Under that, a bar per day split by the groups the breakdown lists, in the same colours, and the breakdown itself by what started it, Bot, what it bought or model; tapping a row narrows the page to it, and each narrowing is a chip that widens again. The most expensive Turns sit beside the breakdown on a wide screen and under it on a phone, each opening its Bot's conversation. `GET /api/billing/spending` also serves the trigger, conversation and Plugin dimensions, which the page does not offer.

The dimensions are the Bot that spent it; **what started it** — a chat with a Bot, a Routine, a Group Chat, voice, mail to a Bot, the person using a Computer directly, or a Plugin's own page; the trigger (the person, email, a schedule, a webhook, a connected app, run by hand); the conversation; what it bought (model replies, conversation summaries, web search, Computer time); the model; and the Plugin that made the call.

**What started it is the start of the chain, recorded when the charge is made.** A hand-off, a subagent task or a question to another Bot is charged to whatever started the Turn that asked, so a Routine is charged for the work it sets going in other Bots too. Within one Bot the chain is read back from the run that asked (`app/billing/run-cause.ts`); where it crosses into another Bot or a subagent object, the asking side writes its cause onto the origin (`StoredRunCauseV1`), because the run that asked is not readable from there. A Computer charge is the running Turn's, read from the Bot object the billing proxy sits in; viewer and control time is always the person's.

Attribution is descriptive. It sits in `billing_attribution` beside the operation, it is not part of the reservation's fingerprint — a retry that names a renamed Routine is still the same charge — and a malformed one is recorded as unattributed rather than refusing the call. A Routine's and a Group Chat's name are kept as last charged, so a rename reads by its new name.

The previous period's total, the per-day split and the biggest cause come from the same rollup; the credit's pace is the account's last seven days, whatever the view, because the credit is account-wide, and a deployment that does not bill reports no credit at all. Every settlement adds itself, in the settlement's own transaction, to an hourly rollup (`billing_spend_hourly`) and to its Turn's running total (`billing_spend_runs`); `GET /api/billing/spending` reads only those, never the operation log. A rollup that fails leaves a gap in the page, never an unsettled charge. The rollup starts empty on deploy; charges settled before it are in the usage list only. Hours rather than days, because the person's days are in their Profile timezone: the rollup is summed into them when it is read. A Turn has one Bot, cause, trigger and conversation, so a grouping by those counts Turns; categories, models and Plugins split a Turn, so a view narrowed by them lists no Turns and counts none.

## Daily limits and spike alerts

A person can give any Bot or Routine a **daily limit** from its row under Spending (`POST /api/billing/limits` with a scope of `bot|<botId>` or `routine|<botId>|<routineId>` and `dailyMicros`, or `null` to remove it). A day is the person's own, from midnight in their Profile timezone.

A limit stops background work, never the person: a charge whose cause is a chat, a Group Chat, voice, the person on the Computer or a Plugin's own page is never refused by one, and a Bot's limit counts only its background spend, so the person's own chatting cannot use it up. Every other attributed charge is held to its Bot's limit, and a Routine's charges — in whichever Bot its chain reaches — to the Routine's. A day starts at the person's midnight, daylight saving included; where it starts on the half hour, the hourly rollup rounds it up, so the first minutes go uncounted rather than yesterday's last ones holding a limit past midnight. The check is inside the reservation's own transaction and on what has settled today, so the charge that crosses a limit completes and the next one is refused with `DAILY_LIMIT_REASON_V1`, which a failed Turn shows as written. A charge already reserved is never refused again: its retry is the same charge. An unattributed charge is held to nothing.

Before each firing, a Routine asks whether its own or its Bot's limit is reached. The first firing a limit stops in the person's day — before it runs, or part way through — fails with a message in the Bot's conversation, the same message and push as any Routine that did not run. Only once that message is written does the Routine note the day, and the rest of that day's firings are skipped quietly, so a Routine that runs every half hour says so once. An account with no limits answers the check without reading anything else.

After a firing that finishes, the Routine asks for a **spike**: a day at least three times its average over up to the previous seven days it has existed, and at least US$0.50. The account hands the alert to one firing a day, which posts it on its own run as a message — "has spent US$0.91 today, about 4× its usual US$0.22 a day" — through the same path. A Routine with fewer than three days of history has no usual yet and raises none.

## Stripe

The hosted deployment's payments Package is `app/payments/stripe`. It pins Stripe API version `2025-02-24.acacia`. Configure the webhook destination to the same version. Secrets remain in the Worker environment and never enter the client, Bot context, Workspace or Sprite.

- One Stripe customer per User, with `metadata.frockbot_user_id`.
- Three monthly recurring USD prices, 500 cents (BYO), 2000 cents (Standard) and 5000 cents (Plus); interval `month`, quantity one.
- Checkout creates subscriptions or one-off card top-ups using server-owned amounts.
- Checkout/customer intents are stored before Stripe writes. Requests use stable idempotency keys. An unresolved write is not retried past Stripe's safe deduplication window; it requires reconciliation.
- Webhook signatures use HMAC-SHA256 over the unmodified raw body, a five-minute timestamp tolerance, constant-time cryptographic verification, and a 1 MiB body limit.
- The gateway resolves customer ownership through Stripe's customer metadata, then the User object checks that the customer matches its stored customer.
- Webhooks retrieve canonical Stripe objects; receipt/state/grant writes are atomic. A changed account revision during an external read forces an event retry rather than a stale overwrite.
- Only a paid recurring invoice for a plan's full price grants its monthly allowance and advances paid access. A trial's free invoice grants the trial credit alone. Subscription `active` or `trialing`, or a checkout redirect, does not authorize spending by itself.
- Top-up credit is granted once per paid Checkout Session, with currency/amount/customer/intent validation.
- Failed renewal payment cannot grant credit. Cancellation preserves the already paid window and stops the next renewal. Refund/dispute events suspend further paid work pending review.
- A repeat purchase command with different values conflicts rather than silently changing the amount.
- Deleting the account deletes its Stripe customer: the one the ledger recorded, and any a search by `metadata['frockbot_user_id']` finds, since a creation whose answer was lost leaves one the ledger never saw. Stripe cancels a deleted customer's subscription at once, without proration, so what is left of the paid period and any credit is forfeited — the confirmation says so. A `DELETE` is idempotent at Stripe and takes no idempotency key, and an answer of `404` is a deletion. With payments unconfigured but a customer recorded, the step is refused and retried rather than skipped, because finishing around it could leave a subscription charging an account that no longer exists. Stripe keeps its own record of payments already made. The webhook answers `received` for an event about a deleted customer, or one that reaches an account already deleted, so Stripe does not retry it for days.

Its routes are `/api/billing/provider/checkout` (a subscription or top-up Checkout Session), `/api/billing/provider/portal` (the Customer Portal), `/api/billing/provider/plan` (a [plan change](#plans-and-the-trial)) and `/api/billing/provider/webhook`. It answers the same at the addresses in use before the seam — `/api/billing/checkout`, `/api/billing/portal` and `/api/billing/stripe/webhook` — because the registered webhook endpoint is configuration in Stripe and an installed app updates on its own schedule. Register `/api/billing/provider/webhook` (or keep the existing `/api/billing/stripe/webhook`) for:

- `invoice.paid`
- `invoice.payment_failed`
- `customer.subscription.created`, `.updated`, `.deleted`
- `checkout.session.completed`, `.async_payment_succeeded`
- `charge.refunded`
- `charge.dispute.created`

### Creating a plan's price

Create a plan's product and price with the `stripe` CLI, in test mode and then live. FrockBot's Stripe account is not the CLI's default, so pass its project. BYO was created this way:

```bash
stripe --project-name frockbot products create --name="FrockBot BYO" -d "metadata[frockbot_plan]=byo"
stripe --project-name frockbot prices create --product= id -d "recurring[interval]=month" --lookup-key=frockbot_byo_monthly --nickname="BYO monthly" --unit-amount=500 <product >--currency=usd
```

The live CLI key may write prices but not products; a live price can carry its product inline instead:

```bash
stripe --project-name frockbot prices create --live --unit-amount=500 --currency=usd -d "recurring[interval]=month" -d "product_data[name]=FrockBot BYO" -d "product_data[metadata][frockbot_plan]=byo" --lookup-key=frockbot_byo_monthly --nickname="BYO monthly"
```

`--lookup-key` makes a second creation fail rather than duplicate, and `stripe --project-name frockbot prices list --lookup-keys frockbot_byo_monthly` finds the id again. The live id is the `production` environment's GitHub secret, which `release.yml` passes to the deploy (`gh secret set STRIPE_BYO_PRICE_ID --env production`); staging receives no Stripe values from the release, so its test id is put on the staging Worker directly (`wrangler secret put STRIPE_BYO_PRICE_ID --name frockbot-cloudflare-staging`).

Configure Stripe Customer Portal for payment-method updates, invoices and cancellation at period end. Plan changes go through the `change-plan` action (`POST /api/billing/provider/plan`), never the portal; do not enable plan switching, quantities, prorations or coupons there. Trials are set per Checkout session, not on the price. Auto-top-up is not implemented; purchases are explicit.

## Reconciliation

An authenticated deployment admin may POST `/api/billing/reconcile` with a target `userId` and a `command` containing a unique `id`, an evidence-bearing `reason` (10–1000 characters), and a `settlement` for a previously reserved operation, a `revokeGrantId` for refunded credit, or an explicit `suspended` boolean. The gateway records the authenticated admin as `actorId`. Revoke refunded credit before lifting suspension; a revoked grant remains expired even when a later operation releases an old hold. The operation's recorded reservation is the maximum customer charge; reconciliation cannot create a new unreserved debit. Commands retain their reason in a durable receipt and reject conflicting retries. This route is not offered to Bots or ordinary Users.

An uncertain Stripe checkout/customer creation is investigated in Stripe by its recorded idempotency key/metadata. Do not issue a fresh creation blindly. A failed response is not proof that Stripe did nothing.

## Sprites metering investigation

Verified on 9 September 2026:

- [Fly's current Sprite prices](https://fly.io/sprites/) charge measured CPU and RAM, not just VM uptime.
- [The Sprite design article](https://fly.io/blog/design-and-implementation/) describes underlying Fly Machines, but does not promise customer access to their Machines API app/ID pair.
- [The machine-events billing advice](https://community.fly.io/t/machine-metrics-query-and-app-usage-report/7094) is from September 2022, before Sprites, and concerns ordinary Fly Machines.
- [Fly's Sprite metrics discussion](https://community.fly.io/t/support-for-multi-tenant-account-billing-sprites-api/27207) says Sprite metrics were not exposed. The [later per-Sprite usage request](https://community.fly.io/t/programmatic-usage-tracking-for-sprites-api-or-webhook-for-billing-data/27523) has no published solution.
- A read-only request to our actual `GET /v1/sprites?max_results=1` returned Sprite ID, organization, status, lifecycle timestamps and URL fields; no `machine_id` or app identifier. The SDK exposes a machine ID on its _restart_ result, but restarting customer computers to discover a hidden backend is neither a stable mapping nor a verified source of billable resource measurements.

Start/stop times alone cannot give cumulative CPU time, integrated RAM use or storage bytes. FrockBot therefore does not describe the launch tariff as measured resource billing. The approved fallback is a published fixed active-time tariff priced from the full Sprite ceiling.
