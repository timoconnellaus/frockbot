Density
Theme

Small, quiet, and the same everywhere

FrockBot sits beside Slack, Linear and the terminal. Its interface should read like those products: a 13px working size on a pointer device, 15px on a phone, controls no taller than they need to be, and one accent spent where it matters.

Every value on this page is a named token. Flutter gets the same names with the same numbers. If a component here and a widget there disagree, the token is the referee.

Principles

Five rules that decide most questions before they are asked.

Density is a token, not a redesign.Two densities, compact and touch. Same names, different pixels. A screen never mixes them.
Labels sit above fields.No floating labels, no outlined Material fields. Label, control, help line, in that order, always.
One accent, spent once per view.The primary action is pink. Nothing else is. Status uses its own good/warn/danger hues.
Tone before lines.Three steps of surface: window, group, tile. A phone list is one lighter group with tiles inside it, no hairlines. Desktop keeps 1px borders; shadows are for things that float.
Small text, quiet weight.Messages at 15 on a phone, chrome at 14 or below, nothing under 20px heavier than 600. Big friendly text reads as a toy.
Dark is home, light is real.Dark is the product default. Light is a full palette, not an inversion, because Applets and email previews need it.

Density

Flip the switch in the rail. Compact is the desktop shell and any pointer device. Touch is the phone and the Flutter app. Control heights grow from 28/32/36 to 28/34/40, text steps up one pixel, the tap target is 44px even where the drawn control is smaller, and nothing else changes. The phone reference is ChatGPT and iMessage: messages at the body size, chrome you barely notice.

Why two and not a slider. Every screen is either operated with a pointer or a thumb. Two fixed sets keep the Flutter port a table, not a formula.

Where the phone got bulky. Material 3 gives a 52px filled button, a 56px app bar, 16px padding inside everything and a card around everything. Touch density keeps the tap target at 44px but draws a 36px control, puts hairlines between rows instead of gaps, and uses no cards inside a list.

Rhythm does not change. The 4px spacing scale, radii and motion are shared by both densities.

Ten roles, no more. A screen picks from these; it never sets a size. The style linter already rejects literal font sizes in plugin CSS, and the Flutter theme will expose the same ten as named text styles.

Weight carries hierarchy, gently. Titles 700, headings and rows 600, body 400. Nothing below 20px is heavier than 600; Manrope at 700 goes chunky at small sizes. Size differences between neighbours stay small so the page reads calm.

Text has three tiers of contrast, 92%, 62% and 45% of the text colour, not three greys. Pure white on the darkest ground reads loud.

Numbers are tabular. Captions, counters and timestamps set tabular-nums so lists line up.

Balanced headings. Display and title text wrap with text-wrap: balance; Flutter gets the same by keeping those strings short.

Spacing & shape

A 4px scale. On desktop, controls are 10px radius, cards 14, panels 20. On a phone, buttons and chips become pills, groups 16, icon tiles 11, and fields stay at 10. Shadows appear on floating things and on secondary pills only.

Inside a control: horizontal padding is pad.control, the gap between icon and text is gap.inline. Vertical padding is never set; the height token is.

Between controls: 8 in a row, space.row between fields, 16 between groups, 24 between sections. Lists never use gaps: rows touch, separated by a hairline.

Screen edges: space.edge, 16 on a phone, 20 on desktop panels.

Motion: 140ms for state changes, 260ms for things entering. Reduced motion turns both off.

Buttons

Four variants, three sizes. Primary is the one pink thing on a view. Secondary is bordered and raised. Ghost is for toolbars and rows. Danger is soft until hovered, so a destructive action is legible but not shouting.

Phone · touch density

Text is semibold, size base. A button's label never exceeds the body text beside it. That single rule is most of the difference from the Material default.

Shape follows density. Desktop buttons are 10px rounded rectangles. Phone buttons are pills, and a secondary pill carries a light shadow instead of a border.

Height is the token. sm/md/lg map to control.sm/md/lg. On a phone a large button is 42px, not 52, and a medium one is 36.

Full width only on a phone, and only for the one primary action at the bottom of a form or sheet.

Press feedback is a 3% scale for 140ms and a darker fill. No ripple.

Disabled keeps the shape and drops to disabled text on subtle surface. Never 50% opacity over content.

Fields

Label above, control, help below. The label is the small bold role; help is a caption. Focus is a 1px accent border plus a soft ring. Error swaps both for danger and puts the reason in the help line.

No floating labels. They cost a top margin, clash with placeholders and cannot hold a counter. The counter lives in the label's right slot.

Entered text is text.input: 13 on desktop, 16 on a phone so iOS does not zoom.

Height is control.md: 32 desktop, 36 phone. Textareas take the same padding and a 2.4× minimum.

Select is the same control with a chevron. Native pickers on the phone, a menu on desktop, identical at rest.

Errors go in the help line, in danger colour, in a sentence that says how to fix it.

Selection

Checkbox, radio and switch share the accent fill and the focus ring. A switch is a row: label left, control right, the whole row tappable.

18px box on desktop, 22 on a phone. The row is control.md tall with a 44px tap area, so a thumb finds it without the list looking padded.

Radio groups replace dropdowns whenever there are five or fewer options that fit on screen.

Chips & badges

A chip is a small pill that names a state or a filter. A badge is a count. Status chips use the status hues; the accent chip marks the selected filter.

“Quotes are curly,” ranges use an en dash (9–5), and a unit keeps its number: 6 pm, 2 s, 400 KB.
Disabled is the same control at 40%

Type grid. Every role has a pixel line height on the 4px grid, so stacked text in two columns lines up. Tracking tightens as size grows.

Light, not shadow. A raised group carries a 1px top highlight at 6% white. Dark surfaces get lighter as they come forward, and the ground is a touch cooler than the surfaces on it.

Status is desaturated in the dark. Good, warn and danger sit 15% lower in chroma so they read without glowing.

Accent at three strengths. Solid for the one action, 14% tint for tiles and selection, and an ink tone for tinted words. Solid pink never colours text.

Icons match text weight. 1.6px strokes beside 400 text, 1.75 in buttons, and glyphs nudged half a pixel so they sit on the baseline.

Dividers start at the text, never under the tile. Tiles and avatars use a radius of 27% of their size so every size is one family.

Selection, caret, scrollbar take the brand: accent at 24% for selection, accent caret, thin border-tint scrollbars, no overscroll flash.

Phone screens

Three screens at touch density, built only from the pieces above. Rows live in one tonal group with icon tiles, the app bar is 44px, actions are pills, and the composer is a pill. The home screen borrows one idea from the voice-app reference: a greeting whose key numbers are tinted in the accent.

What is next

Once these foundations are agreed, the same page grows in this order, and each step lands in the repo as the single source both platforms build from.

1. SurfacesCards, list rows, dividers, app bar, bottom sheet and dialog, empty states with the display face.
2. ChatMessage bubbles at body-large, the composer, the pending and stopped states, tool-call rows.
3. Tokens filepackages/plugin-ui-theme becomes the source: one JSON of tokens, generated CSS for the shell and Applet kit, generated Dart for Flutter.
4. Flutter portA FrockTokens theme extension and widgets that mirror each class here, then side-by-side screenshots from the Pixel and a browser at 390px to confirm they match.
5. Applet kit and A2UIThe kit stylesheet and A2UI component set consume the generated CSS, so an Applet looks native in both hosts.
